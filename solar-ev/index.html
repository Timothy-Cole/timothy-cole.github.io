The numbers

"Effective cost" is what you pay for grid power, minus feed-in credit, plus the value of any battery charge you'd be short at the end compared with the best option (priced at the off-peak rate).

How it works

For every hour from now until the end of tomorrow it simulates your house: solar first powers the home and car, the surplus fills the battery, and anything left is exported. When solar runs short the battery covers the home (down to your reserve), then the grid.

  • Solar comes from the Open-Meteo forecast of sunlight on a panel at your roof's angle, which already accounts for cloud. It's scaled by your panel size and performance, and capped at your inverter limit.
  • House use follows a typical Australian daily pattern (small morning bump, big evening peak) scaled to your daily total.
  • Charge from battery: the car starts charging at plug-in time and the battery supplies it. Charge from grid: the car charges in the off-peak window and the battery is left for the house (set your inverter or charger to stop the battery feeding the car).
  • It's a forecast, so treat close calls as close calls. Cloud forecasts more than a day out are rough.
