      Settings
      Bedtime stays within 30 minutes of her usual bedtime. Settle is the time from lights out until she's asleep.
      How it works

      Tonight's bedtime. The planner starts from two rules and takes the later one:

      • Sleep need. About 12 hours in 24 hours, naps included, for a 3-year-old. You can change this in Settings. Night sleep is that minus the nap, with a minimum of 10h, counted back from the wake-up target.
      • Time awake after the nap. About 5 hours after a nap ends before she's ready to sleep.

      The result is then kept within 30 minutes of her usual bedtime. A steady bedtime helps her body clock, so no-nap days go to the early end of that range and late naps to the late end. It's never before 6:00pm.

      Wake her by is the earliest of: the 1h 30m nap cap, 5 hours before her usual bedtime, or the point where more nap would push bedtime past her usual time.

      Wired. A toddler who's wired at bedtime is often overtired, not undertired. After no nap, a nap under 45 minutes, or more than 5h 30m awake, the planner suggests keeping bedtime and calming things down instead of moving it later. After a decent nap, Wired moves bedtime 15 minutes later.

      Learning her patterns. From the last 14 on-time nights (lights out less than 15 minutes after the plan), the planner looks at three things:

      • Settle time (lights out to asleep). 15–20 minutes is ideal. Regularly 30 minutes or more suggests she isn't tired enough yet, so a slightly later bedtime may help. Under 10 minutes suggests she may be overtired.
      • Early waking (30 minutes or more before the wake-up target). Regular early waking usually means bedtime is too late, not too early.
      • Morning mood. Regular grumpy mornings suggest she needs more sleep. Her total sleep before happy mornings shows what she really needs.

      Early waking or grumpy mornings point to an earlier bedtime. Long settling points to a later one. When both show up together, it usually means too much daytime sleep. With the switch on, the plan moves by 15 or 30 minutes.

      Ready to drop the nap? If she settles 15 minutes or more faster on no-nap days, the planner suggests trying quiet time instead.

      Catch-up routine. If you start late, books are cut down to 15 minutes, then the bath to 10 minutes, keeping the same order. If she's also wired after a good nap, each Wired tap still adds 15 minutes of books.

      Sources: the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the US National Sleep Foundation give 10–13h per 24h for ages 3–5, and the NHS gives about 12h at age 3. Awake times, settle times and the early-waking signs are common advice from sleep consultants, not official guidelines. This is a guide only. Your child's own cues come first.
